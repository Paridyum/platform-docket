# MKT-0004 — The Indian armed forces welfare estate, and where it fails

## Why this artifact exists

C-0006 requires that every vertical Paridyum enters be recorded in an artifact naming the evidence
that selected it, and that the evidence not be tenant-derived. `product-registry.yaml` turns that
into a dossier obligation: `kind: catalogue` at `stage: proposed` owes an `MKT-`.

This is that artifact for `veer-entitlements`, and it is also the evidence base for C-0009 and
GLO-0010. **None of it is tenant-derived** — Paridyum has no tenants, and every source above is
external, public and dated.

## The finding, in one sentence

**The welfare estate for Indian armed forces families is large, old, well-funded and
well-intentioned; its characteristic failure is not absence of entitlement but inability to reach
it, and the failures that reach the press are clerical.**

## What exists

The structure is a century old and it is not small.

**Kendriya Sainik Board**, an attached office of the Department of Ex-Servicemen Welfare under the
Ministry of Defence, established 1919 and named as it now is since 1975. Beneath it, **34 Rajya
Sainik Boards** at state and Union Territory level, each chaired by a Chief Minister, and **410
Zila Sainik Boards** at district level, each chaired by a Collector. Establishment costs are split
between Centre and State 75:25 for special category states and 60:40 elsewhere.

Alongside it: **DESW** for pensions, **ECHS** for health, **DGR** for resettlement, the **Armed
Forces Flag Day Fund** (constituted 1993, administered by KSB, Managing Committee chaired by the
Defence Minister) for medical treatment, mobility equipment, home loan interest subsidy, education
for children of the fallen and vocational training for widows — and, separately, each state's own
ex-gratia and land schemes, and the Ministry of Home Affairs' provision for the CAPF.

**This is not a market with a funding gap.** Anyone approaching it as one has misread it, and a
company that arrives offering to raise money is offering the thing the estate is least short of.

## Where it fails

The failure is at the seams, and the two documented cases below are cited as *instances of a
mechanism*, not as a measurement.

**Thirty years.** Kanta Katoch, widow of Major K C Katoch who died in service in 1989, was still
awaiting Special Family Pension when reported in January 2021 — after the Armed Forces Tribunal
allowed her petition in 2018 and the Government of India directed release. The proximate causes
were: a Pension Payment Order sent to a bank branch in Ludhiana rather than to the Defence Pension
Disbursing Officer in Chandigarh; a bank that would not acknowledge receipt; and, once redirected,
a DPDO that said it had a photocopy rather than the original.

**Eleven years.** Suksham Sharma was paid Special Family Pension rather than Liberalised Family
Pension because her husband's death was classified as a physical casualty, although his Commanding
Officer had declared it a battle casualty. She petitioned in 2014 and the Armed Forces Tribunal at
Chandimandir found the unit had been in an operational area on the International Border in
Rajasthan.

**The mechanism, stated as a claim that could be wrong:** entitlement in this estate is determined
by a classification and delivered through a routing, and both are performed by different
authorities across the MoD, the disbursing agencies, the banks and the state boards. A family
cannot see the state of either, has no single authority accountable for the whole path, and
discovers a failure years later as an amount that did not arrive. **The tribunal is functioning as
the reconciliation layer**, which is an expensive place to put one.

**Bound on the claim.** Two press-reported cases are not a base rate. What the sources support is
that this failure mode exists, is not rare enough to be unreportable, and survives court orders.
What they do not support is any statement about how many families it affects. **No claim about
prevalence may be made in Paridyum's copy, pricing or product design on the strength of this
artifact.** Establishing prevalence would need RTI data or a study, and neither has been done.

## The gap that is Paridyum's shape

**DGR's self-employment schemes are, without exception, physical-asset attachments**: the Security
Agency Scheme, management of CNG stations, coal loading and transportation, and oil product
agencies. Elsewhere the resettlement estate adds milk and produce booths and Janaushadhi generic
pharmacies. Each is a real livelihood and each is a *thing* — a tipper, a forecourt, a booth, a
shop.

**Nothing in the self-employment estate is a software, IT or digital-services business.** DGR does
train in corporate management, cyber security and technology domains through IIMs and NIELIT — but
training is not a livelihood scheme, and a certificate is not a business.

That is the observation with the sharpest edge for Paridyum, and it cuts in a specific direction.
The estate hands over an asset and no operating layer. C-0009 obligation III hands over the
operating layer and no asset — the runtime, the business semantics, the isolation guarantee, the
export path — to a population the estate has already identified, verified and issued documents to.
**The two are complements rather than competitors**, which is worth stating because the reflex
reading of this section is that Paridyum should build a rival to DGR, and that reading is wrong.

## What Paridyum owes, statutorily

**Nothing.** Section 135 of the Companies Act 2013 binds a company meeting any of: net worth ₹500
crore or more, turnover ₹1,000 crore or more, or net profit ₹5 crore or more in a financial year —
at 2% of average net profit over three preceding years. Paridyum meets none of these and is not
yet incorporated.

Schedule VII clause (vi) names this beneficiary class as an eligible CSR activity, and the MCA
notification of 23 June 2020 extended it to CAPF and CPMF veterans and their dependents including
widows. **That is relevant to how the vehicle is structured (RFC-0026) and irrelevant to whether
the obligation exists (C-0009).** The distinction is worth holding: an artifact that justified the
covenant by pointing at Schedule VII would be justifying it as anticipated compliance, and the
whole content of C-0009 is that it is not.

## Vocabulary

Recorded here because it is an external fact with a source rather than a house style choice.

An Army Headquarters circular advised against "martyr" on the ground that the word denotes someone
killed for religious or political belief, and named eleven terms to use instead: **Killed-in-Action,
Laid down their Lives, Supreme Sacrifice for the nation, Fallen Heroes, Indian Army Braves, Fallen
Soldiers, Battle Casualty, Bravehearts, Braves whom we lost, Veergati / Veergati Prapt, Veer.** The
Ministry of Home Affairs clarified in December 2013 that "shaheed/martyr is not defined anywhere"
and that it was issuing no such order or notification; the Ministry of Defence informed the Home
Ministry in 2015 that the word is not used in reference to any of the casualties.

**Bound:** this rests on press reporting. The circular itself and the parliamentary record have not
been read. The direction is unambiguous across three independent statements and the specific dates
should be re-verified before they are quoted externally.

## What would change this artifact

An `MKT-` is an observation and is superseded by better evidence, never locked. Specifically:

- RTI or audited data establishing prevalence would replace the bound in "Where it fails" with a
  number, and is the single most valuable thing that could be added.
- A DGR self-employment scheme in software or digital services would remove the gap this artifact
  identifies, and would be good news that costs Paridyum its differentiator.
- Digitisation of the pension and classification path — a single accountable status view across
  MoD, the disbursing authorities and the state boards — would close the mechanism directly, and
  should be watched for, because building against a gap the state is closing is how this kind of
  product wastes years.
