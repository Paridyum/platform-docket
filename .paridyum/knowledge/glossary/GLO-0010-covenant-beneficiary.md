# GLO-0010 — Covenant beneficiary

## Definition

**A covenant beneficiary is a person in one of the tiers below, or a business majority-owned
or operated by such a person. The tiers are a priority order, and they bind when capacity, money
or attention is scarce.**

**Tier 1 — Next of Kin of the fallen.** The Veer Naris, children, and dependent parents of
personnel of the Indian Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Air Force) recorded as **Battle Casualties**,
and of the Central Armed Police Forces and Central Para Military Forces recorded as **Operations
Casualties**. Also: the Next of Kin of personnel who died in service in circumstances the relevant
service recognises as attributable to service.

**Tier 2 — The disabled.** Veterans invalided out of service with a disability attributable to or
aggravated by service, and their dependents.

**Tier 3 — Those who served.** All other ex-servicemen and CAPF/CPMF veterans, and their
dependents.

**Every tier covers both ministries**: the Armed Forces under the Ministry of Defence, and the
CAPF and CPMF — CRPF, BSF, ITBP, CISF, SSB, Assam Rifles — under the Ministry of Home Affairs.

## Why the order is written down

The tiers are not a ranking of worth. They are a rule for the moment the covenant cannot serve
everyone, which is every moment before Paridyum is large.

An undifferentiated definition looks more generous and behaves worse. Under load it is resolved by
whoever is asking, which in practice means whoever is best placed to ask — and the population
Tier 1 describes is, by MKT-0004's evidence, the one least equipped to advocate for itself and the
one waiting eleven and thirty years on paperwork. A definition that does not name a priority order
has one anyway; it is simply unwritten and therefore unchallengeable.

## Why both ministries

The word the country uses does not follow the ministry boundary, and neither does the loss.

Excluding the CAPF would exclude the families that **Bharat Ke Veer** — the Ministry of Home
Affairs fundraising initiative most Indians associate with the phrase — exists to serve. The
Ministry of Corporate Affairs reached the same conclusion for a different reason: Schedule VII
clause (vi) of the Companies Act 2013 originally named armed forces veterans, war widows and their
dependents, and was extended by notification on 23 June 2020 to include CAPF and CPMF veterans and
their dependents including widows. MKT-0004 carries both sources.

**Accepted cost of the wider boundary:** it is roughly an order of magnitude more people than the
Battle-Casualty-only reading, and obligation III of C-0009 is uncapped. The priority order is the
mechanism that makes the width survivable, which is why the two decisions are in one artifact
rather than two.

## The words this repository uses

**"Martyr" and "shaheed" are not used.** The Ministry of Home Affairs clarified in December 2013
that "shaheed/martyr is not defined anywhere" and that it was issuing no such order or
notification; the Ministry of Defence informed the Home Ministry in 2015 that the word is not used
in reference to any of the casualties. An Army Headquarters circular gave the reason: the term
denotes someone killed for a religious or political belief, which is not what happened to people
killed in the course of duty.

**Bound on this claim**, carried from MKT-0004: it rests on press reporting. The circular and the
parliamentary record have not been read directly. The direction is consistent across three
independent statements; the dates should be re-verified before being quoted externally.

The terms that circular named, and the ones used here: **Killed in Action**, **Battle
Casualty**, **Operations Casualty**, **Laid Down Their Lives**, **Supreme Sacrifice**, **Veergati
Prapt**, **Veer**, **Bravehearts**, **Fallen Heroes**. Families are **Veer Naris** and **Next of
Kin**.

This entry exists partly to hold that vocabulary, so it is retrieved rather than reinvented by
each person and each agent that writes copy, a schema field, or a support macro.

## What it is not

**It is not a serving-personnel definition.** Serving members of the Armed Forces and CAPF are
outside every tier. They are employed, in-service welfare is their employer's obligation, and
a private company offering them commercial benefits raises a conflict question this covenant has
no reason to walk into.

**It is not self-declared.** Eligibility rests on documents the Indian state already issues — the
ex-servicemen identity card, the Zila Sainik Board / Rajya Sainik Board record, the PPO, the
service pension record, the discharge book. Paridyum verifies against those and does not invent a
parallel registry. REQ-0020 carries what this means for the tenancy model.

**It is not a discount tier.** A beneficiary tenant is a tenant, at the same isolation guarantee,
under C-0008's rule that no party on the runtime is a privileged shape. The grant is on price, and
on nothing else.

**It is not `tenant`, `account`, or `organisation`.** Those are tenancy positions (GLO-0002,
GLO-0004). This is an attribute of a person, and of a business through the person who owns or runs
it. One beneficiary may hold several tenancy positions and the attribute travels with none of
them automatically.

**It is not restricted to India by accident.** It is restricted to the Indian Armed Forces and
Indian central police and paramilitary forces on purpose. C-0009 is a specific commitment to a
specific institution, not a general policy about veterans everywhere, and widening it later is an
amendment upward under C-0009's barrier — permitted, and never accidental.
